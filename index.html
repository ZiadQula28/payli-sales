Five design directions for the Payli sales website and company profile.

Each option is a matching pair: a website with the interactive pay-at-table demo, and a PDF company profile in the same style. The facts, claims and wording are identical in all five; only the design changes. Pick the direction you want, and that pair becomes the final version.

How to review: open each website and scroll it on a laptop and on your phone, run the live demo (the "Auto-play" button narrates it), then open the PDF. All five PDFs were checked in three different PDF engines (Apple, Chrome and MuPDF) so they look the same in Preview, Chrome, Acrobat, mail apps and on phones.

Option 01

Luminous

The dark, glowing deck look from the team's moodboard, refined: mint light fields, glass cards, floating product, a giant wordmark.

Feels: premium fintech, cinematic, confident. Best for: investors, launch events, screens in a dark room.

Option 02

Editorial

Light paper, magazine typography, hairline grids, numbered chapters and figure captions. Mint used once per page, as a highlighter.

Feels: calm, exact, trustworthy, printed. Best for: owners who read, finance teams, leave-behinds that get printed.

Option 03

Studio

Product first: a soft grey canvas with white rounded tiles, the real screens as the hero, friendly type, quiet mint halos. In the spirit of an Apple product page.

Feels: clean, modern, approachable. Best for: showing the product itself; owners who want to see the software, not a pitch.

Option 04

Blueprint

A deep-teal drafting sheet: grid paper, mono labels, a system schematic, "failure modes" and "compatibility matrix" tables, a title block on every page.

Feels: engineered, precise, honest. Best for: operations and finance people, POS partners, anyone who asks "how does it actually work?"

Option 05

Poster

Bold colour fields in Payli's mint, teal and cream, giant uppercase headlines, thick rules, stickers and marquee strips. A brand campaign rather than a brochure.

Feels: energetic, consumer, memorable. Best for: cafés and younger venues, social media, events, standing out in a crowded inbox.
