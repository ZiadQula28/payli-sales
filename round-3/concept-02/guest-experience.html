The restaurant’s side. On this page it stays empty.

Nothing to install. Nothing to sign up for.

Your guest scans a QR code with the phone already in their hand. A page opens in the browser, in English or Arabic. This page shows every screen they see.

At the table

Pay at Table, closed beta

Six screens. The guest moves forward when they are ready, not when someone is free to bring the bill.

Six screens. Swipe sideways to follow them.

  1. 1Scan

    The guest points the camera at the QR on the table.

  2. 2Bill

    The itemised bill, with service charge and tax, in JOD.

  3. 3Split

    Pay it all, or share it: evenly, by item, or an amount.

  4. 4Tip

    5%, 10%, a custom amount, or none.

  5. 5Pay

    By card or wallet, on the payment provider’s own page.

  6. 6Paid

    A confirmation on screen. A rating, if the guest wishes.

No surprises at the table

Guests see exactly what they will pay before they pay it. Each guest pays a share from their own phone. Nobody does arithmetic at the table, and nobody waits for the card machine.

Cedar & Sage and Table 12 are sample data. Cedar & Sage is a fictional venue used for illustration.

At the counter

Order Pager, in development

Four screens on one page. The guest sits down instead of hovering at the counter, and follows the order from the seat.

Four screens. Swipe sideways to follow them.

  1. 1Order received

    “We’ve got your order”, with the order number and a time estimate.

  2. 2Preparing

    The timeline moves when staff start the order. The estimate stays in view.

  3. 3Ready

    “Your order is ready”, and where to collect it.

  4. 4Collected

    “Enjoy your order”. The page has done its work.

The alert, part by part

The guest can turn on sound and notifications once. Both are the guest’s choice.

The page updates live. It chimes when sound is on and, on Android, vibrates. With permission it also shows a notification. It follows the phone’s own sound settings.

If the estimate passes, the page says “Taking a little longer than expected”. It never invents a number.

Order Pager is in development and not yet live at any venue. Order #24 is sample data. Cedar & Sage is a fictional venue used for illustration.

Two languages

English and Arabic on every guest screen. The guest chooses. The restaurant dashboard is in English today.

English

The order page as a guest reads it in English. The switch to Arabic sits at the top of every screen.

Arabic

The same moment in Arabic. The page reads right to left. The restaurant sets the wording of each status in both languages.

What the guest is never asked for

Four things other systems ask of a guest. Payli asks for none of them.

An app

The QR code opens a page in the phone’s browser. Nothing is downloaded and nothing is updated.

Pay at Table and Order Pager

An account

No sign-up and no password. No Wi-Fi password either.

Pay at Table and Order Pager

A phone number

The order page itself is the pager. The guest gives no phone number to be alerted.

Order Pager, in development

Card details, by Payli

Card details are entered on the payment provider’s own secure page. They never reach Payli.
